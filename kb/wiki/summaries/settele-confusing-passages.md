# When a Passage Feels Confusing

Confusion is a signal to stop rereading indiscriminately. First identify the relationship the question tests—main idea, purpose, evidence, contrast, cause, or inference—then return to the small amount of text that bears on that relationship.

## Procedure

1. Read the question and name its task in plain language.
2. Use a dumb summary to represent the passage's basic movement.
3. Map the relevant entities and relationship; ignore technical labels until they are needed.
4. Find the sentence or transition that establishes the relationship.
5. Test answer choices against that local evidence and their exact scope.

The strategy is question-driven, not passage-avoidant: the passage remains the authority, but rereading the entire passage is replaced by targeted retrieval and logical mapping.
