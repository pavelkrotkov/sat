# Trap Answers

SAT wrong answers are often not absurd. They typically contain a small mismatch: an unsupported strong word, a reversal of the passage's relationship, a true statement answering a neighboring question, an overgeneralization, or a detail that does not address the prompt.

## Elimination checklist

- What exact question is being answered?
- Does every part of the choice follow from the passage, or did it add a new claim?
- Is the scope too broad or too narrow?
- Is cause/effect, comparison, or viewpoint reversed?
- Is the choice true but irrelevant to the requested purpose?
- Does one loaded word make the whole choice too strong?

Treat each choice as a compound claim: one unsupported clause is enough to eliminate it. Use the question's wording as the constraint rather than rewarding a choice for merely mentioning passage keywords.
