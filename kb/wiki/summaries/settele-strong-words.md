# Strong Words

A strong word materially raises what the passage must prove. In answer choices, inspect words that make a claim absolute, certain, exclusive, causal, or broader than the passage. A choice can sound generally right yet fail because one strong word adds an unsupported commitment.

## Procedure

1. Identify the answer's core claim.
2. Circle or mentally isolate its strongest word or phrase.
3. Ask whether the passage explicitly supports that degree of certainty, scope, or causation.
4. Prefer the weaker wording when both choices express the same supported idea and the weaker one adds fewer commitments.
5. Do not reject a choice merely because it uses a weak word; weak wording can be correct when it accurately matches the passage.

Strong words are about evidentiary force, not only positive or negative connotation. This tactic complements [[summaries/settele-dumb-summaries]] and explains why many plausible choices are traps.
