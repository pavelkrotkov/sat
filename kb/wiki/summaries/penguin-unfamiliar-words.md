# Unfamiliar Words

When answer choices contain unfamiliar vocabulary, do not begin by trying to recognize a dictionary definition. First predict the meaning or function the sentence requires from its contrast, cause, tone, and surrounding details. Then compare each choice against that prediction.

## Procedure

1. Cover or ignore the choices briefly.
2. State the sentence-level meaning needed in ordinary words.
3. Check grammar and part of speech.
4. Test unfamiliar choices in the sentence, rejecting any that break the local logic or tone.
5. Use the passage's relationship—not familiarity with the word—as the deciding evidence.

This turns a vocabulary-looking item into a context-and-logic problem. It is especially useful in a harder second module, where three choices may be obscure.
