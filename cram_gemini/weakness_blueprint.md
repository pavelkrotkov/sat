### Step 2: The Strongest Patterns in Your Mistakes

You are missing highly specific, advanced concepts. Here are the recurring traps you are falling for:

#### Math Patterns

* **Trap 1: The Discriminant Blindspot.** You consistently miss questions asking for "no real solutions" (Test 4 Q13, Test 10 Q20). You need to immediately trigger $b^2 - 4ac < 0$ whenever you see those words.
* **Trap 2: Vertex Form Translation.** When a word problem gives you a maximum/minimum or a turnaround point, you struggle to build the equation $y = a(x-h)^2 + k$ (Test 4 Q22, Test 9 Q20).
* **Trap 3: 2D Unit Conversions.** In Test 9 Q21, you converted square feet to square meters but forgot to *square the conversion factor* (1 meter = 3.28 feet, so $1 \text{ m}^2 = 3.28^2 \text{ ft}^2$). This is a classic SAT trap.
* **Trap 4: Algebraic Function Mapping.** You struggle when the SAT nests functions or transforms them, like $g(x) = f(x)/(x+3)$ (Test 6 Q20) or $g(x+28)$ (Test 11 Q21).
* **Trap 5: Standard Deviation Intuition.** Test 10 Q8 shows a misunderstanding of spread. Remember: data clustered in the middle means *low* standard deviation; data clustered at the edges means *high* standard deviation.

#### Reading & Writing Patterns

* **Trap 6: "Logically Completes the Text" in Hard Science.** You missed 8 of these (Tests 4, 6, 7, 8, 11). You are likely getting lost in the scientific jargon (e.g., Narwhals, Titan's methane, S. kowalevskii acorn worms) and missing the core logical relationship (cause/effect or contrast).
* **Trap 7: Punctuation with Interrupting Modifiers.** You consistently miss questions testing commas around non-essential phrases (Test 6 Q19, Test 8 Q19, Test 10 Q19). If you can cross out the phrase and the sentence still works, it needs commas on *both* sides.
* **Trap 8: Subject-Verb Separation.** The SAT loves to put a plural noun right before a verb when the actual subject is singular, way back at the start of the sentence (e.g., "the shape... is", Test 8 Q20; "recipes... epitomize", Test 6 Q15).
* **Trap 9: Semicolons vs. Comma Splices.** You are choosing comma splices when a full boundary is needed (Test 4 Q17, Test 5 Q20).
* **Trap 10: High-Tier Vocab & Secondary Meanings.** You are missing hard tier-2 words (sanguine, stymie, tenuous, paucity) and secondary definitions of common words (disputing = providing resistance to).
* **Trap 11: Ignoring the Specific Constraint in Rhetorical Synthesis.** In the bullet point notes questions, you are picking answers that are true to the notes but *fail to answer the specific prompt* (e.g., failing to emphasize a sequence or a contrast).

### Step 3: 1-Week Cram Priority Ranking

If you only have one week, do not study everything equally. Attack the concepts with the highest Return on Investment (ROI)—things that are heavily rules-based and easy to fix in a few days.

| Priority | Category | Action Item for the Week | Impact Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Grammar (Boundaries) | Drill Semicolons vs. Periods vs. Comma Splices. Remember: Semicolons and periods are mathematically identical on the SAT. | **Ultra-High** (Instant points) |
| **2** | Grammar (Verbs) | Practice crossing out prepositional phrases to match the subject directly to the verb. | **Ultra-High** (Instant points) |
| **3** | Math (Discriminant) | Memorize the 3 rules for $b^2 - 4ac$: $>0$ (two real), $=0$ (one real), $<0$ (no real). Apply this to any "no solution" quadratic. | **High** (Guaranteed to show up) |
| **4** | Math (Vertex Form) | Drill writing $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ from word problems. Plug in a second point to solve for $a$. | **High** |
| **5** | R&W (Rhetorical Synthesis) | Before reading the bullet points, read the *question*. If it asks for a contrast, eliminate any choice without a contrast word (however, while). | **High** (Quick strategy fix) |
| **6** | Math (Unit Conversions) | Review 2D and 3D conversions. If $1\text{m} = 3\text{ft}$, then $1\text{m}^2 = 9\text{ft}^2$. | **Medium** |
| **7** | R&W (Logical Conclusions) | When reading heavy science passages, ignore the big words. Map it as $A \rightarrow B$, but $C \rightarrow D$. What happens to $A$? | **Medium** (Takes more practice) |
